# Yellow Zone Research Summary: Fold-Out Garden Market

*All research for the 1UP Yellow Zone project in one place. Last updated Nov 18, 2026. Sources are linked inline and listed at the end.*

## 1. Bottom line

- The Evergreen Brick Works has flooded **three times in about a dozen years** (2013, July 16, 2024, Sept 2, 2026). Its flood design protects the buildings, **not the stock on the ground floor**.
- The team's chosen solution is the **Fold-Out Garden Market**. A small reserve of stock lives in a cabinet at a dry host outside the floodplain, and during a closure the cabinet folds open into a counter so customers can still pick up reserved orders.
- **Budget: CAD $100,000, website capped at $100.** Year one costs **$81,104.23** (hall at the higher $200/h rate, 10% contingency), the website $100, and **$18,795.77** is held as a year-two reserve. See [BUDGET.md](BUDGET.md). (The earlier $61,330.51 plan is superseded.)
- **Biggest unknown:** the proposed host, Copp Hall, has not agreed to anything, and its current prices are not published.

## 3. The problem we are solving

- **Raised storage alone can fail.** Outward Bound Canada, a tenant at the Brick Works, raised its gear about 3 ft after the 2024 flood. In Sept 2026 the water rose higher, got into its containers and knocked over its shelving ([Outward Bound, 2026](https://www.outwardbound.ca/blog/outward-bound-canada-toronto-flood-2026/)).
- **Protected stock can still be unsellable.** Even when goods survive, the shop is closed for days or weeks, so vendors and Evergreen lose sales.
- **Evergreen already does** sponge-style drainage (cisterns, pervious concrete) and four-season programming (the Koerner rink). Those ideas were dropped as duplicates.

## 4. The chosen solution: Fold-Out Garden Market

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Cabinets | 2 freestanding, wheeled cabinets, 900 x 550 x 1,250 mm. One at the Garden Market on ordinary days, one stocked year-round at the dry host |
| Fold-out | A 450 mm front panel pivots down on a hinge about 800 mm high, and two recessed legs lock with braces to make a counter. Washable trays stay at display height |
| Safety | Brakes, braced legs and a clear approach are checked before loading. Nothing is fixed to heritage walls. Nobody re-enters a closed site for stock |
| Stock | Light, high-value reserve only (seeds, books). Rotated through ordinary pop-ups |
| Test | 4 paired drills against a plain folding table and bins. If the cheap setup works as well, use it |

What we kept from the earlier shelf plan: staff approve every public message, washable trays, the vendor checklist, a manual fallback and timed drills.
