# Fold-Out Garden Market
## Fold-out pickup pilot chosen for the competition

Can a vendor complete a pickup safely when the Yellow Zone Garden Market is closed? A small reserve of seeds and books would stay year-round at a host outside the mapped river floodplain. The same cabinet holds the goods and opens into a selling counter. One cabinet serves the Garden Market on ordinary days and a second lives at the host. A flood closure never requires a trip back into the market.

### What the two plans contribute

Ethan’s shelf plan gives staff control over public updates, gets people out before anyone handles goods, keeps lightweight items together in washable trays and records drill results. Keep those practices. One tradeoff matters: a well-designed raised shelf can protect more on-site goods per dollar, but preserved stock can still be inaccessible. The off-site market can fulfil a selected order during that closure, but it needs a willing host and cannot protect the remaining shop inventory.

Evergreen already documented raised retail displays and a staff weather tool, and safely evacuated in September 2026. Outward Bound Canada reported that water overtopped its own raised storage. Its experience was in a tenant space. A limited off-site trial gives us a way to measure the additional service and compare it to a much cheaper table and bins. ### Storage and sales

Prototype envelope: 900 mm wide, 550 mm deep and 1,250 mm tall. Park on a level surface and lock the wheels. Release the front latches. Side stays control the 450 mm panel as it pivots down around a hinge about 800 mm above the floor. Swing down both recessed legs and lock their braces. Check stability before loading the counter. The trays remain in the cabinet at display height. A qualified reviewer and actual users must test reach, clearances, stability, pinch points and load limits. Rotate reserve stock at ordinary pop-ups. Reuse trays and replace individual hardware. No attachment to historic walls is proposed.

### First app demo

1. **Maya** publishes the main site's closure and separately approves a host address, access instructions and hours. All public messages have a timestamp. The host stays closed to reservations until ready.
2. **Aisha** opens the cabinet and completes a checklist: brakes set, legs braced, approach clear and stock counted. She confirms the pickup window and 12 seed packets.
3. **Daniel** opens a link or QR code, sees the accessible entrance and reserves one packet. One reservation reduces availability to 11. At the stall he presents a code and pays. Staff can enter a phone reservation through the same inventory.
4. **Marcus** shares the same public link with workshop guests. Opt-in notifications lead to that page. Any later AI assistance draws only on approved hours, access and pickup rules.
5. If access or connectivity is uncertain, pause new reservations. Reconcile existing holds, collections and paper transactions before starting again. Evergreen staff retain authority over closures and re-entry.

Suggested first contribution for Claude: a mocked mobile web flow from staff approval to vendor count to customer pickup. Synthetic stock only. Test closed or paused locations, simultaneous attempts to claim the last item, retrying a reservation or collection, and reconciliation after an outage. Live gauge integration, public display hardware and alarm wiring are outside this proposal. A missed stock task cannot trigger an evacuation alarm.

### Pilot decision

Months 1–2: confirm vendor demand, verify the host's dry storage and event access, obtain quotes and map the accessible route. Months 3–4: build one cabinet, test it and run four paired trials against a table and bins; alternate the order of the two setups. Release the second unit after safety and usability review. Months 5–12: run regular pop-ups and record the time to first correct pickup, stock discrepancies, labour, open hours, missed pickups and annual carrying cost. If a table and bins perform as well, use the simpler equipment. If the host cannot offer short-notice access, retain scheduled pop-ups but revisit the flood-continuity claim.

### Proposed host and budget

Copp Hall at Rosedale United Church has hosted vendor markets and offers lift access. Its floor plan shows storage rooms on the hall’s level. The address sits outside the mapped river floodplain. The lower-level room still needs a water-entry history check, secure storage, cabinet clearance, retail permission, an agreed opening procedure and an actual quote.
