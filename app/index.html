        Plain day-by-day accuracy is 97.7%, but a model that never warns scores 97.0% because high water is rare. That is why the event counts above are the honest measure. These scores assume a good rain forecast for tomorrow; real forecasts miss some cloudbursts, so expect a little worse.

        Scored on 2020 to 2023 against "high water today or tomorrow". Toronto's humidity passes 75% on about 9 days in 10, flood or not, so the rule warns almost every day. A model built on humidity alone catches only 7 of 20 events a day ahead, with 44 false-alarm days a year. Humidity in the hours before rain carries a little signal, so the model keeps it as one input. Rain today and tomorrow, the last few days of rain and snowmelt do most of the work.

        Method and limits

        Question. Will the Don at Todmorden (gauge 02HC024) average 20 m³/s or more today or tomorrow? That level was reached on 178 days from 2000 to 2023, about 7 a year. The July 2013 flood averaged 53 m³/s.

        Inputs. Rain today and tomorrow (forecast) and the two days before, a 0.85-per-day fading total of past rain (how soaked the ground is), heaviest hourly rain, snowmelt, warmth, peak humidity, humidity in the 5 hours before the heaviest rain, pressure swing and season.

        Data. Rain from Environment Canada stations (Toronto City, North York, Pearson). Other weather from the Open-Meteo archive. River flow from the Water Survey of Canada. All free, no keys.

        Training. Logistic regression trained on 2000 to 2015, alert levels picked on 2016 to 2019, scored once on 2020 to 2023. The shipped version is refit on 2000 to 2023. Retrain with node model/train.mjs.

        After 2023. Official daily flows stop at 2023, so later floods were checked by hand, using measured rain. The day before the 16 July 2024 flood scored 95% (Warning). The day before 17 August 2024 scored 28% (Watch). The day before 2 September 2026 scored 16% (Watch, just over the line).

        Limits. Training used the rain that actually fell tomorrow, so in daily use the model is only as good as the rain forecast, and summer cloudbursts are the hardest to forecast. It works by day, so it cannot promise an exact 5-hour lead. Pair it with the live gauge card and the hourly chart, and treat Watch as "get ready".
