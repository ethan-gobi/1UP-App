## Cost Efficiency & Impact

**How does your spending maximize environmental and social impact?**
Over half the budget goes to physical protection that works every day without power: raised, latched cabinets and reusable trays that keep reserve stock out of the water. We reuse Evergreen's existing carts and keep the site's wet-floodproofing, drainage, and planted areas untouched. The digital spend makes sure staff, vendors, and visitors get the same approved warning by app, text, and alarm, so nobody stays behind to save stock. Reusable trays, repairable parts, and a screen that sleeps after hours cut waste and energy.

**What partnerships, donations, or in-kind contributions will help reduce costs?**
Evergreen staff time for drills and training, existing carts and trays, and the floor space (in-kind). TRCA's free real-time flood monitoring and floodplain mapping replace paid data. We will ask cabinet suppliers for pilot or educational pricing, and Garden Market vendors to test the trays and app for free. *(None of these are confirmed yet. Say "will ask" unless someone has agreed.)*

**How will you sustain or scale the project after initial funding?**
The pilot already pays for year one of hosting, texts, and maintenance. From year two, Evergreen covers roughly $4,000 a year in running costs out of operations, or splits it with Garden Market vendors through their stall fees. The app and alarm are built once, so adding a cabinet costs about $7,000 and adding another yellow-zone building (Welcome Centre, Koerner Gardens, TD Future Cities Centre) mainly costs cabinets, not software. Drill results give Evergreen the evidence to apply for climate adaptation grants to expand.

## Weak spots, plainly

1. **Contingency breaks the template rule.** $16,000 is 19% of costs; the template allows 5–10%. Judges will notice. Fixed above.
2. **Three empty categories.** No outreach, travel, or admin money. Permits and insurance for a heritage building in the TRCA floodplain are real costs, and texts only work if people sign up. Fixed above.
3. **$7,000 per installed cabinet is unproven.** A custom, engineered-restraint, flood-suitable cabinet with a lift mechanism, installed in a heritage building, for $7K including tax is optimistic. No quote backs it. Say "allowance, pending quotes" and point to the contingency.
4. **Software outweighs protection.** $26–28K on the app versus $14K on the two cabinets that actually keep stock dry. Expect the question "why not more cabinets?" Answer: the app is reused across every future cabinet and building, so its cost per cabinet falls as you scale.
5. **Tiny protected capacity.** iMove-type shelves are rated about 8 kg each. Two cabinets hold very little stock, and the plan never states how much (kg, trays, or dollars of inventory). Add one line, e.g. "protects about X trays, worth about $Y of stock", or judges cannot judge value for money.
6. **Year-two money is not secured.** The sustainability answer depends on Evergreen paying ~$4K a year. Nobody has agreed to that. Keep the wording as a plan, not a fact.
7. **Partnerships are wishes.** No partner has signed on. Write "will ask" rather than "will provide".
