| **Benefits** | Reserve stock is stored high every day, including after hours, so protection does not depend on last-minute action. One removable tray works as the display, the cart load and the stored unit. Staff move a whole tray instead of repacking each item. Cabinets stand clear of historic walls, and the manual shelves work without power. | Evergreen says raised critical systems, drainage and monitoring helped the site drain and recover faster after the 2026 flood (Evergreen, 2026). Kesseboehmer iMove shelves bring high storage down to reach and include a loading lock. Pilot measures packing time, staff needed, handling steps and protected capacity against the current setup. Cost: $100,000 total, including an $18,000 contingency. **Interactive:** [TRCA real-time flood monitoring](https://trcagauging.ca/) (live rain and river levels); [Don River at York Mills live gauge](https://www.riverapp.net/station/563f4b8d6123fe1ffe2c1726); [Toronto flood resilience map](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/d4a088fd0d95440383ee4872a66498a5) (TMU student project: where water collects, drainage, green space). |
| **Limitations** | Covers light, water-sensitive goods only. Heavy pots, soil, large plants and refrigeration are outside the pilot. A large enough flood can rise above any shelf height. Staff can stow stock only while access is safe. At evacuation, handling stops. The pilot covers up to four cabinets, not every building or all stock. | iMove published load rating: up to 8 kg per mechanism. In Sept 2026, water rose above Outward Bound's storage, which had been raised about 3 ft (Outward Bound, 2026). Budget allows $28,000 for up to four cabinet modules. Storage height must be set from local flood modelling and surveyed floor levels. **Interactive:** [TRCA Flood Plain Map Viewer](https://trca.ca/conservation/flood-risk-management/flood-plain-map-viewer/#map) (Brick Works sits in the Don floodplain); [TRCA Regulated Area viewer](https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/1a3f466a3d014cd8aac79421df381c53) (changes need approval). |
| **1 Case Study** | **Outward Bound Canada at Brick Works.** After the 2024 flood it raised equipment about 3 ft and used waterproof containers. In Sept 2026 the water rose higher, got into the containers and tipped over the shelving. It lost much of its equipment and expected no access to its space for at least September. | Source: [Outward Bound Canada, 2026](https://www.outwardbound.ca/blog/outward-bound-canada-toronto-flood-2026/). Lesson for our design: set the shelf height from site modelling, restrain the cabinets, and move goods out early when a flood could top the shelves. **Interactive:** [TRCA Flood Plain Map Viewer](https://trca.ca/conservation/flood-risk-management/flood-plain-map-viewer/#map) shows how much of the site the Don can reach. |
