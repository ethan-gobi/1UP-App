# Yellow Zone: 8 Solutions

Built Maya-first (staff), then checked against Daniel (family visitor), Aisha (Garden Market vendor) and Marcus (event organizer). A **Gap** line means the solution does not fully serve that person, so pair it with another solution.

Context: the Garden Market flooded July 16, 2024 and the site flooded again September 2, 2026 (after 2013). The $15.6M Evergreen Brick Works 2.0 revitalization is underway.

---

## 1. Live Flood Status Board + Text Alerts
**Idea:** Water-level sensors in the Don Valley and on site feed one status (Green / Yellow / Red) shown on a screen at the Welcome Centre, the website, and opt-in SMS.
**Zone:** Welcome Centre (hub), sensors site-wide.
- **Maya:** one source of truth for closing, reopening and telling the public.
- **Daniel:** checks before leaving home, no wasted trip with kids.
- **Aisha:** gets early warning to move stock.
- **Marcus:** can warn guests and switch plans hours ahead.
- **Gap:** warns people but protects nothing physically. Must pair with #2 or #3.

## 2. Raised, Rolling Market Shelving + Flood-Lift Storage
**Idea:** Garden Market fixtures on lockable wheels plus a raised mezzanine or upper-shelf "safe zone" so stock can be lifted above flood height in under 30 minutes.
**Zone:** Garden Market.
- **Aisha:** directly protects inventory, the thing she lost in 2024.
- **Maya:** fast, rehearsed evacuation of goods, quicker reopening.
- **Marcus:** rolling fixtures clear the floor for markets and events.
- **Daniel:** indirect only (market reopens sooner).
- **Gap:** does nothing for visitor safety or access. Weak for Daniel.

## 3. Deployable Flood Barriers at Building Entrances
**Idea:** Slot-in or self-rising flood gates at Garden Market, Welcome Centre and Future Cities Centre doors, with a staff deployment drill.
**Zone:** Garden Market, Welcome Centre, TD Future Cities Centre.
- **Maya:** core flood-safety infrastructure she asked for.
- **Aisha:** keeps water out of the market, fewer closure days.
- **Marcus:** protects the venue and bookings.
- **Daniel:** protects the buildings, not his route there.
- **Gap:** needs staff on site to deploy; useless for a surprise overnight storm like Sept 2, 2026 unless self-rising.

## 4. Raised Boardwalk "Dry Route" with Clear Wayfinding
**Idea:** A raised, stroller- and wheelchair-friendly boardwalk linking parking, Welcome Centre, Garden Market and Koerner Gardens, with colour-coded signs showing the safe route in wet weather.
**Zone:** Connects all four Yellow Zone spaces.
- **Daniel:** safe, navigable path with kids in any weather.
- **Maya:** clearer crowd flow, less liability, easier communication.
- **Aisha:** customers can still reach the market after heavy rain.
- **Marcus:** guests arrive dry; route doubles as event procession path.
- **Gap:** expensive and needs heritage and conservation approval. Does not stop flooding.

## 5. Sponge Garden: Rain Gardens + Permeable Paving in Koerner Gardens
**Idea:** Turn hard surfaces around Koerner Gardens into rain gardens, bioswales and permeable paving that soak up stormwater before it reaches buildings, with signs explaining how it works.
**Zone:** Koerner Gardens, edges of Garden Market.
- **Maya:** reduces flood volume, nature-based and fits Evergreen's mission.
- **Daniel:** a hands-on learning spot for kids.
- **Marcus:** a green, photogenic event backdrop.
- **Aisha:** less water at the market door.
- **Gap:** helps with everyday heavy rain, not a 100-year storm. Cannot be the only defence.

## 6. Flood Recovery Kit + 48-Hour Reopen Playbook
**Idea:** Pre-staged pumps, dehumidifiers, cleaning supplies and a step-by-step staff playbook with roles, so each zone has a target reopening time.
**Zone:** Stored in the Welcome Centre, used everywhere.
- **Maya:** this is exactly her "quick recovery time" goal.
- **Aisha:** shorter closures, faster back to selling.
- **Marcus:** a dependable reopening date to give clients.
- **Daniel:** site reopens sooner.
- **Gap:** all behind the scenes. Visitors never see it, so pair with #1 to tell them.

## 7. Pop-Up Market and Event Backup Plan
**Idea:** When a building floods, the Garden Market moves to a covered pop-up in Koerner Gardens or the Future Cities Centre, and events shift to a pre-booked backup space, with an online shop for vendors in the meantime.
**Zone:** Garden Market, TD Future Cities Centre, Koerner Gardens.
- **Aisha:** keeps selling during closures; the 2026 Farmers Market already had to move to the Distillery District.
- **Marcus:** a guaranteed Plan B, so he can book with confidence year-round.
- **Maya:** a ready plan instead of scrambling.
- **Daniel:** still has something to visit.
- **Gap:** depends on the backup space staying dry. Needs #3 protecting the Future Cities Centre.

## 8. Four-Season Covered Hub at Koerner Gardens
**Idea:** A retractable canopy and heated wind-screen panels over part of Koerner Gardens, so it works as a family space, event venue and skating rink in rain, heat or snow.
**Zone:** Koerner Gardens.
- **Daniel:** year-round access with kids, shelter from storms and heat.
- **Marcus:** a weatherproof, adaptable venue all year.
- **Aisha:** more foot traffic in off-seasons.
- **Maya:** a sheltered gathering point during alerts.
- **Gap:** costly; does nothing about floodwater. Must sit above flood height.
